How to create an SOP

Write instructions someone can follow without guessing.

  1. Choose one repeatable workflow.

    Use a specific title such as “Publish the weekly project status,” rather than something broad like “Manage projects.” Define the beginning and end.

  2. Identify the owner and reviewer.

    Record who maintains this document, the approval authority, version number, and effective date. Processes change; the SOP needs an owner.

  3. List prerequisites.

    Include tools, permissions, relevant training, inputs and safety requirements. Do not put real passwords or sensitive customer data in screenshots.

  4. Use short instructions with expected outcomes.

    Start each step with a verb: Open, Select, Review, Save. Describe what a person should see after completing the action.

  5. Test the steps with a first-time reader.

    Have someone follow the process without coaching. Correct missing details, confusing branches and outdated screenshots.

  6. Approve, publish and review.

    Record the approved version, change history and review date. Make sure users can find the latest copy.

Completed example

Example SOP: Publish a weekly project update

This fictional example shows the level of specificity worth aiming for. Substitute your organization's actual interface labels and requirements.

SOP ID: OPS-004   Owner: Project coordinator   Version: 1.0

Purpose: Publish a consistent project status update every Friday so stakeholders can review progress and outstanding risks.

Prerequisites: Access to the project tracker, current task status, and permission to publish in the project workspace.

  1. Open the project tracker. Select the active project. Verify the project title at the top of the page.
  2. Review current tasks. Filter for the current reporting week. Note completed, overdue and blocked items.
  3. Create the weekly update. Add the reporting date, completed work, next steps, and blockers.
  4. Review accuracy. Compare any numerical claims against the tracker and remove personal or confidential information.
  5. Publish and record the link. Share with the approved group; confirm that the new update appears in the project workspace.

Completion check: An approved update is visible in the workspace and its URL is recorded in the team log.

Exception: If data is missing, mark the update as pending and notify the project lead rather than guessing.

Frequently asked questions

Is the SOP template actually free?

Yes. Download Word, PDF or plain text without registering. The optional TeachAtlas browser extension has separate Free and Pro plans.

Can I edit the PDF?

The PDF is primarily for printing. Choose the Word template for easy editing in Word or compatible software.

Is a checklist the same as an SOP?

A checklist confirms selected actions. A complete SOP also explains purpose, scope, roles, prerequisites, decisions, exceptions, verification and version control.

Does this template satisfy industry regulations?

Not by itself. Organizations should adapt it to their applicable laws, internal policies and review procedures.
